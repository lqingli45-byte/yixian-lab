Fictional onboarding-checklist project. The names and dates below are teaching material, not a real client record.

1. Capture the source

Raw notes (abbreviated)

  • M2 · June 8 Maya said she would send the updated draft by Thursday, June 11.
  • M3 · June 8 The team discussed a Friday release. The meeting ended without agreeing a release date.
  • M4 · June 8 Alex asked whether legal review is required. The question stayed open.
  • M5 · June 11 Maya sent the draft. Alex confirmed receipt.
  • M6 · June 11 Alex approved using the draft for internal review only. Release timing and legal review remain open.
2. Verify, then track

What can be recorded

Action A-001 — Maya, send the updated onboarding-checklist draft, due 11 June 2026. Status: Done, with evidence from M2 and M5.

Decision D-001 — Use the draft for internal review. This is the only confirmed decision.

Not confirmed — a Friday external release date, and whether legal review is required. These stay as a proposal and an open question, not as approved facts.

What this sample is meant to show

  1. Write the dated original first, and label important lines (here, M2–M6).
  2. If you use AI, treat the draft as a starting point and compare it with the source.
  3. Keep confirmed work, proposals, and open questions in different places.
  4. Give each approved action a stable ID, and close it only when you have evidence.
  5. At the end of the week, count only verified results. In this example, one action is done; the release date is still open.
